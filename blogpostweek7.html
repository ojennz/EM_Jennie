In the opening chapters of "The Electronic Disturbance", Critical Art Ensemble consider how power becomes more elusive as institutions we recognise with authority provide us with decreasing opportunity to participate in decisions made on our behalf. "The Virtual Condition" describes the shifting of power as a result of the proliferation of electronic networks. "Nomadic Power and Cultural Resistance" extends this discussion to consider the case of occupying an institution's physical premises while that institution's operations and assets are dispersed over electronic networks. I was reminded of my experience with online job applications when they described the difference between accessing an institution and accessing its decision makers / assets. I have had many instances where I've applied for jobs en masse and received no response. I've also received rejections that I've found myself wanting to interrogate in greater depth. Was there something about my portfolio or work that should have been highlighted more? Did I not convey the right information about my skills and background? Ultimately I learn that I will not be considered further for a role. I then set about crafting a new application for another position, with no insight into my previous application's fate. The online application portal is interesting to me because it differs in the extent of information provided to me prior to and after submission. Prior to submission, I know exactly where I will be inputting my application documents, and after submission, I may have visibility of my progress through the process, but not where or how to query it in the same way. Presumably an individual made a judgement about my work and decided to reject my application at some location. I am, however, afforded minimal information on how I may contact or learn more about this person and their decision. It is a useful mechanism for me to be involved in an institution's recruitment process: I can provide information to the employer, and they can communicate whether I have been rejected or not. Could it be that power does not need to traverse multiple locations to become inaccessible? Critical Art Ensemble's discussion of an occupied building allowed me to understand that by gaining access to one point of an institution, I may not be in a position to impact many others. For my job applications, the only area that I can act on and alter is my own submission. Therefore, it is there that I will focus my efforts. I still hope to improve on my current body of work, but I wonder how many of my efforts are in response to my lack of knowledge about my rejected applications.
