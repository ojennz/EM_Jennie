It is always fascinating to read something written in the past that somehow reflects the future, or, I should say, the present. The Memex sounds similar to many technologies we use today: a system that stores information, allows us to retrieve it quickly, and connects different materials together. But what interested me most was not whether Bush successfully “predicted” the internet. It was the relationship he imagined between human thinking and technology. Bush describes the human mind as working through association rather than through a fixed system of indexing. His idea of the Memex follows this logic: instead of simply storing information, a person could connect different materials and create “trails” through them. In this system, the machine helps preserve and extend a person’s thinking, but the person is still the one creating the connections. This made me think about how I use AI today. When I have a difficult reading, I can upload it to AI and immediately ask for the main argument, important concepts, or even possible interpretations. This is extremely helpful, especially when I am overwhelmed by the amount of information I need to process. But sometimes I realize that I have skipped the process of making connections myself. Instead of reading something, becoming confused, connecting it to another idea, and slowly forming my own interpretation, I receive an organized “trail” almost immediately. This is also similar to how we are taking in information from social media. We are constantly flooded with information that has already been collected and processed into the quickest possible format for us to consume. Technology is developing so fast that sometimes, instead of helping people process information, it adjusts the information itself to fit our increasingly shorter attention spans. In a way, we are no longer only using technology to organize information. The way information is organized by technology is also changing the way we think. But considering the reality, it is impossible to completely reject technology while the world continues to grow so quickly with it. One way I have come up with for myself is to have small “detox” sessions by reading printed books and continuing to journal by hand, just to leave some space for my brain to process without technology. In a book I recently read, I also found some habits that I think can help us organize our thoughts better. In short, write down action items, keep writing down ideas that pop up as “backburners,” and set a routine to come back and review them once in a while. These are very simple things, but I think that is also the point. While we are adapting to AI and all these new technologies, at the end of the day, we may still need to go back to some of the most basic ways of thinking in order to reconnect with ourselves.
