Donna Haraway's A Cyborg Manifesto was full of interesting phrases that I attributed to her desire to bring multiple, conflicting positions together in her writing. However, I stopped to consider one phrase in particular in "The Informatics of Domination"; in a table, she presents: "Perfection → Optimization". I had always thought of 'optimization' as an innocuous term - to improve an existing process or system. What it obscured, and what I didn't question before, is: optimisation towards what? Haraway outlines a number of principles of 'regime of calculable power', whereby she discusses how power can be exercised over subjects and their actions by defining regimes and limits by which they are to operate. Seeing this table reminded me of how readily I adopt similar principles to make decisions. How can I improve/complete a task in less time? How can I make this activity yield more value from the time I spend? These are the questions that arise for me when deciding to do something, often preceding the question of whether I even want to do it in the first place. When I'm deciding to take some time to rest, this manifests in my thought process. I may simply want to spend a period of time resting, but think that I should read some books instead. As I enjoy reading, I will do so, but I do so with a sense of 'optimising' my time, to at least have some form of gain from the 20 minutes that I am resting. The paradoxical thing here is that I have made the decision to take 20 minutes to do nothing, but then seek to determine how to increase the utility of that 20 minutes. So although I may enjoy simply resting, I feel compelled to justify resting by reading instead. I found myself compelled to think about what is driving this compulsion to read and 'optimise' my time. It seems second nature to me to make this judgement on my own time. In Haraway's table, this corresponds to a judgement of whether an activity will result in some form of 'gain'. This then becomes the dominant factor in my decision-making, even for a short period of time that I have designated for myself to do nothing. What am I forgoing by placing this criterion as my first line of thought?
