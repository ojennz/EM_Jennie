It was interesting reading Constitue... I think that there are some things that we have progressed on since this essay was written in 1970. There are some things that we as a society still suffer from when it comes to media though. I believe that we already have achieved what he is saying to some extent. Now that almost everyone has access to technology, they have the ability to record their lives, post about their thoughts and express their own views on events that are happening around them. But as mentioned before **just because you have the power to express yourself does not mean that you have the ability to influence others. ** Just because we have the power to create content doesn't mean that we have the power to share our content to others. As we have learned from social media companies we still rely on them to decide what we see and don't see. Something else that I have been thinking about lately is how we get opinions. Many times we come across articles that have an opinion and we tend to spread those more. Not to say that opinion based articles are bad, but I think that there is less digging into topics these days. There are certain topics that you should take your time to learn about. But if someone has already told you what they think you will just believe that person and move on. Do we even have opinions on things these days? Or do we just pick from opinions that others have already decided for us? As mentioned in the article we will always have some kind of manipulation in media. The only difference would be who is controlling it. I feel as if we will never know what really happened during certain historical events. Because while we may not be able to control what people see we can control what they read about first. An example of this would be when learning about the history of colonialism we always hear about Columbus discovering America first. Then we learn about the natives and the bad things that happened to them. So I don't know if having the ability to know everything will lead us to be more informed as a society. Maybe we just want to hear what we want to hear regardless of what algorithm you use.
