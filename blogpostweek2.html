The part that interests me the most is when the reading talks about how AI uses intimacy to capture us. It makes me think about how, when AI first started entering our lives, it was mainly a tool that could make our work and thinking more efficient. Then, it gradually progressed into companionship and even something like personalized therapy. It has endless patience, is there 24 hours a day, and isn't affected by your emotions, while still seeming to understand you. For a while, I relied a lot on talking to AI because I felt that pouring out my negativity or confusion might burden other people, and they might not be able to answer all my questions. I also started seeing a lot of content online from people who seem to feel the same way. But this reading made me start thinking about the difference between communicating with AI and communicating with other people. When we communicate with people, they respond to us with their own emotions, experiences, and their relationship with us. They aren't simply there to serve or please us. AI, on the other hand, responds based on huge amounts of data and is designed to provide a service to us. I think we should be more aware of this difference, because relying too much on this kind of one-sided companionship might affect how we understand real relationships between people. The point mentioned at the end about haunting and capture is also something I have been thinking about. After reading and understanding more about AI, I think the most important thing is not to prohibit ourselves from using it, but to think more carefully about how and why we use it. Before, whenever I had an idea or a first draft, I would immediately want to put it into AI and see how far it could push my idea. I think this came from wanting to see something established in a short amount of time. Now, especially in my creative process, I want to start asking myself, “Why am I using AI here?” I really like how this reading made me notice how quickly I accepted AI and allowed it to become part of my everyday life. I also liked the example of Leymusoom at the end because it doesn't create fear around using AI. Instead, it leads us to think more carefully about how and why we choose to use this technology.
