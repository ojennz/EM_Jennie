This article has led me to reflect on my usage of AI and other technologies. Like the author, I would be wary of the extensive effects on our thought processes and perceptions of all kinds from advances in technology. But I would go further and say there are many benefits as well as negatives, according to particular uses. I was particularly intrigued by the description of “Broken Windows” and “Unbroken Windows”. The former involved people smashing windows over and over so that the AI would learn to identify this particular noise. It struck me as very clear example of people serving at the mercy of a technology. There are so many aspects of daily life where we take it for granted that some technology will be beneficial to us and make things easier, and fail to consider all the people involved in producing this, and its impact on us. I am also reluctant to dismiss greater efficiency in all cases. As English is not my first language, there can be quite a lot of effort involved in comprehending an article. It would be very useful to be able to use AI to gain a general idea of more demanding material in a fraction of the time, allowing me to read more widely. I may well take several hours over an article, and find it hard to concentrate for such a period of time. So here I would benefit greatly from AI, widening the range of material I could read and digest. On the other hand, I accept that to some extent I may be relying on AI's interpretation and so not truly understanding the article in question, with no real idea how much it may have been paraphrased and reduced. So to some extent I wonder to what degree I am forming my own views. So it seems to me it is less a question of using or not using AI, and more a question of how to continue to maintain a degree of scepticism about information we are given and thought processes. Clearly it can be extremely useful to have ready access to information, but be alert to the possible drawbacks. To continue to direct our own thinking, and decide what we wish to learn about and how deeply, perhaps.
